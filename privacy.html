Privacy, in plain words

Vaani, the AI note taker agent: the web app and the Chrome extension. Last updated 4 November 2026.

Short version: Vaani has no account, no server and no tracking. Your notes stay in your browser.

The Chrome extension

  • On Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and Zoom web pages, it checks the meeting’s address and whether a “Join” or hang-up button is showing, so it knows a meeting is open. It does not read your messages, names, video or anything else on the page.
  • Other call apps, only if you turn them on: WhatsApp Web, Slack huddles, Webex, Jitsi Meet, Whereby and Discord. Chrome asks you first, for each site, and each has its own switch in the Vaani popup. There the extension checks only whether a call is in progress: the call’s own end-call control, or a call’s live sound playing in the page. It reads no messages, chats, names, numbers or contacts, and sends nothing anywhere.
  • When a call starts, it asks once, “Take notes with Vaani?”, and points at its icon. Nothing is recorded until you click the Vaani icon (or press its keyboard shortcut). That click opens the Vaani app in a small window, and the app starts taking notes.
  • Hearing the call, with no screen sharing: your click on the icon is what lets Chrome give the Vaani app the sound of that one call tab (Chrome’s tab capture). The extension hands the app a one-time code for it, which works only in the Vaani window that the extension opened itself, and only for a few seconds. The extension itself never records, keeps or sends any sound. While Vaani hears the tab, Chrome shows that the tab is being captured, and the Vaani app plays the call’s sound back to you so you still hear it. When the call ends, the tab goes to another page, you turn off “Hear the whole call”, or the extension is turned off, reloaded or updated during the call, Vaani stops hearing the tab and keeps only your microphone. If Vaani is already taking notes (for example from Google Calendar), the icon on the call tab asks whether to add the call to those same notes, and says when they started; it never starts a second recording. If those notes are in another tab, Vaani moves them into its own window with the call, so you get one note. The Vaani app talks only to the official Vaani extension.
  • While Vaani takes notes, a small bar on the call page says so and has a Stop button, and the Vaani icon shows “REC”.
  • It stores a few settings in Chrome: whether to ask you when a call starts, whether you chose to use only your mic, and whether hearing calls can work for you (a Sarvam AI key is set in Vaani). While Chrome is open it also remembers, in memory only, which tabs are on a call and which Vaani window is taking notes.
  • Chrome asks you first. The permission for hearing a tab is not asked for when you install. The extension asks for it once, the first time you use Vaani on a call, and only when you have a Sarvam AI key (without one, hearing the call can’t work). Chrome’s own message for it says “Read and change all your data on all websites”. The extension uses it only to hear the call tab you clicked on; it reads nothing on any page apart from checking for a “Leave call” button. If you say no, Vaani hears only your microphone that time and asks again on your next call; choose “Use only my mic” and it never asks again. You can turn it off any time: click the Vaani icon and choose “Turn off” next to “Hear the whole call”, or remove it in chrome://extensions (Details, then Permissions).

The Vaani app

  • Speech to text uses your browser’s speech engine unless you add a Sarvam AI key. In Chrome and Edge the browser sends the audio to Google or Microsoft to do this. Safari uses Apple’s engine. Vaani never receives your audio.
  • With a Sarvam AI key set, Sarvam AI does the speech to text: the meeting audio is sent from your browser straight to Sarvam AI (api.sarvam.ai), in pieces of about 25 seconds, to be transcribed, together with your “Names and words” list (up to 50 names and terms) so they are spelled right. Nothing goes to Vaani. The audio is held only in your browser’s memory, never saved, and dropped once it is transcribed. You can switch speech to text back to “This browser” in Settings. Sarvam AI’s own privacy policy applies to what it receives.
  • In the Vaani app for Mac: the Mac app (not released yet) hands this page the call’s sound (your microphone and the call app, mixed, or all Mac sound if you choose that), in memory only, through its own bridge that no other script on the page can use. The page sends it only to Sarvam AI, as above, and tells the Mac app to forget its copy once your notes are saved. Without a Sarvam AI key nothing is sent: the page says a key is needed and keeps the audio in memory until you add one or close it.
  • Hearing a whole call: Vaani never asks you to share your screen. Without the extension it hears your microphone only. With the Chrome extension and a Sarvam AI key, the call tab’s sound is mixed with your microphone inside your browser and sent to Sarvam AI for speech to text, so the other people’s voices go there too. Your browser’s own speech engine only ever gets your microphone. Nothing is saved as audio.
  • After a meeting: Vaani drafts a follow-up email, the tasks and any follow-up meeting in your browser. Gmail (or your mail app), WhatsApp and Google Calendar open only when you tap, already filled in, for you to check and send yourself; Vaani never sends an email or a message for you. The email goes only to the people in the meeting’s calendar invite, or whoever you type in. Slack, only if you add it: if you paste a Slack channel link (a Slack incoming webhook) in Settings, Vaani posts the meeting notes (not the email draft or the transcript) to that one Slack channel when you tap, or on its own after each meeting if you choose “Do it automatically”. Because other pages on the same github.io address share this browser’s storage, Vaani posts on its own only after you chose it in Settings in that same visit, or confirmed the channel on the note, which shows which Slack workspace and link it goes to. The address is kept only in your browser and never goes in a backup. Slack’s own privacy policy applies to what it receives.
  • Notes are written on your device by default. If you add your own AI key (Sarvam AI, OpenAI, Anthropic or Google), text goes to that provider whenever Vaani writes notes, answers a question, recaps your week, translates or drafts a follow-up. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google get text only; a Sarvam AI key also sends the meeting audio to Sarvam AI for speech to text, as above. Ask and Recap send the notes they search. Your “Names and words” list goes along with the notes text so names are spelled right. Your key is stored only in your browser.
  • Saved notes live only in this browser’s storage (local storage, or IndexedDB when that is full). Clearing your browser data deletes them.
  • Insights are worked out in your browser from the notes you saved. Nothing is sent anywhere to make them.
  • Google Calendar, only if you connect it: Vaani reads your next 36 hours of events straight from Google into this browser. Nothing goes to any Vaani server. Disconnect any time. Access is read-only and the token is kept in memory only. Titles, times and call links are kept here for 36 hours, and removed the next time you open Vaani after that. Attendee names and email addresses stay on this device. The email addresses are used only to fill in the “To” line of a follow-up email, or the guests of a follow-up meeting, when you tap; they are never sent to an AI provider. A note you take for an event keeps its title, which goes to your AI provider like any note title if you use your own key. That note also keeps the event’s attendee names, their email addresses and the event id; backups leave them out unless you turn on “Include attendee names and emails in backups” in Settings. Disconnecting deletes the saved events and asks Google to remove Vaani’s access. If the sign-in has expired, Vaani asks Google for a short-lived token only to revoke it. If Google can’t do that without your sign-in, Vaani tells you, and you can remove access yourself in your Google Account under Security, Third-party connections (myaccount.google.com/connections).
  • No accounts, cookies for tracking, analytics or ads.

Your choices

  • Turn off the call prompt in the extension’s menu, or remove the extension at any time.
  • Delete any note from inside Vaani.
  • Disconnect Google Calendar in Settings at any time. You can also remove Vaani in your Google Account under Security, Third-party connections (myaccount.google.com/connections).
