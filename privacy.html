Privacy, in plain words

Vaani, the AI note taker agent: the web app and the Chrome extension (1.3.0). Last updated 19 November 2026.

Short version: Vaani has no account, no server and no tracking. Your notes stay in your browser.

The Chrome extension

  • On Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and Zoom web pages, it checks only whether a call is live: the meeting’s address, whether the call’s hang-up button is showing, and whether the page plays a live call stream (a yes or no; it never listens to it). On Google Meet it also checks whether a participant tile is on the page (a yes or no), so it does not ask while you are still waiting to be let in. It does not read your messages, names, captions, video or anything else on the page, and it sends nothing anywhere.
  • When a call starts, it asks once: a Chrome notification (“Meeting detected. Take notes?”) and a small prompt on the call page, each with Start notes and Not now; “Not now” in either puts both away for that call. Turn “Ask me when a call starts” off in its menu and it stays quiet until you click its icon. Nothing is recorded until you choose Start.
  • Hearing the call, with no screen sharing: a click on the Vaani icon on the call tab (or its keyboard shortcut, Alt+Shift+V) is what lets Chrome give the Vaani app the sound of that one call tab (Chrome’s tab capture). A click on the notification or on the prompt does not count for Chrome, so Vaani then starts with your microphone and tells you that one click on its icon adds everyone. The extension hands the app a one-time code for the tab’s sound, which works only in the Vaani window that the extension opened itself, and only for a few seconds. The extension itself never records, keeps or sends any sound. While Vaani hears the tab, Chrome shows that the tab is being captured, and the Vaani app plays the call’s sound back to you so you still hear it. When the call ends, the tab goes to another page, or you turn off “Hear the whole call”, Vaani stops hearing the tab and keeps only your microphone, and offers to stop the notes.
  • While Vaani takes notes, a small bar on the call page says what it hears and has a Stop button, and the Vaani icon shows “REC” (or “+” while it hears only your mic). The bar can also copy a chat note for the meeting chat, so people know notes are being taken.
  • It stores one setting in Chrome: whether to ask you when a call starts. While Chrome is open it also remembers, in memory only, which tabs are on a call and which Vaani window is taking notes.
  • Chrome asks you first. Its permissions are: storage (that one setting), notifications (the “Meeting detected” and “Your call ended” notices), access to Google Meet, Teams and Zoom pages (the call check above), and, only when you allow it, tab capture (“Hear the whole call”). Tab capture is not asked for when you install: the extension asks once, with the reason, the first time you start notes on a call. Chrome’s own message for it says “Read and change all your data on all websites”; the extension uses it only to hear the call tab you started notes on. If you say no, Vaani hears only your microphone. You can turn it off any time: click the Vaani icon and choose “Turn off” next to “Hear the whole call”.

The Vaani app

  • Speech to text uses your browser’s speech engine unless you add a Sarvam AI key. In Chrome and Edge the browser sends the audio to Google or Microsoft to do this. Safari uses Apple’s engine. Vaani never receives your audio.
  • With a Sarvam AI key set, Sarvam AI does the speech to text: the meeting audio is sent from your browser straight to Sarvam AI (api.sarvam.ai), in pieces of about 25 seconds, to be transcribed, together with your “Names and words” list (up to 50 names and terms) so they are spelled right. A piece with no sound in it is not sent, and the note says how much that was. Nothing goes to Vaani. The audio is held only in your browser’s memory, never saved, and dropped once it is transcribed. You can switch speech to text back to “This browser” in Settings. Sarvam AI’s own privacy policy applies to what it receives.
  • Uploading a recording (an audio or video file you choose) is read inside your browser. With a Sarvam AI key, its sound is cut into pieces of under 25 seconds and sent straight to Sarvam AI (api.sarvam.ai), like a live recording. A quiet recording is made louder before it is sent. A very long recording may be refused on a phone with little memory, before anything is read or sent. Without one, Vaani offers to play it through your browser’s own speech engine, in browsers that allow that. The file is not uploaded to Vaani and is not saved; only the text and the notes are kept on your device.
  • In the Vaani app for Mac: the Mac app (not released yet) hands this page the call’s sound (your microphone and the call app, mixed, or all Mac sound if you choose that), in memory only, through its own bridge that no other script on the page can use. The page sends it only to Sarvam AI, as above, and tells the Mac app to forget its copy once your notes are saved. Without a Sarvam AI key nothing is sent: the page says a key is needed and keeps the audio in memory until you add one or close it.
  • Hearing a whole call: Vaani never asks you to share your screen. Without the extension it hears your microphone only. With the Chrome extension, the call tab’s sound is mixed with your microphone inside your browser. With a Sarvam AI key that mix is sent to Sarvam AI for speech to text, so the other people’s voices go there too. Your browser’s own speech engine only ever gets your microphone, so without a key Vaani says plainly that only your words become text.
  • The audio Vaani keeps in the tab: when your browser does the speech to text, Vaani also records that meeting’s audio (your microphone, and the call if Vaani hears it) into the tab’s memory, so a speech service that fails, or a call where only your mic was turned into text, isn’t lost. It is never written to storage and never sent anywhere on its own. Under the note you can make notes from the whole recording (with a Sarvam AI key it then goes to Sarvam AI, as for an uploaded file) or save it as a file. Closing the tab deletes it.
  • After a meeting: Vaani drafts a follow-up email, the tasks and any follow-up meeting in your browser. Gmail (or your mail app), WhatsApp and Google Calendar open only when you tap, already filled in, for you to check and send yourself; Vaani never sends an email or a message for you. The email goes only to whoever you type in. Slack, only if you add it: if you paste a Slack channel link (a Slack incoming webhook) in Settings, Vaani posts the meeting notes (not the email draft or the transcript) to that one Slack channel when you tap, or on its own after each meeting if you choose “Do it automatically”. Because other pages on the same github.io address share this browser’s storage, Vaani posts on its own only after you chose it in Settings in that same visit, or confirmed the channel on the note, which shows which Slack workspace and link it goes to. The address is kept only in your browser and never goes in a backup. Slack’s own privacy policy applies to what it receives.
  • Notes are written on your device by default. If you add your own AI key (Sarvam AI, OpenAI, Anthropic or Google), text goes to that provider whenever Vaani writes notes, answers a question, recaps your week, translates or drafts a follow-up. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google get text only; a Sarvam AI key also sends the meeting audio to Sarvam AI for speech to text, as above. With the transcript go the meeting’s date and length, the notes you typed while recording, the times you marked and your first name from Settings. If you picked an earlier meeting under “Follow-up to”, that meeting’s title and its open tasks go along too. Ask sends your question with the few passages of your notes and transcripts that match it (at most 8); Recap sends that week’s notes. Your “Names and words” list goes along with the notes text so names are spelled right. Your key is stored only in your browser.
  • Saved notes live only in this browser’s storage (local storage, or IndexedDB when that is full). Clearing your browser data deletes them.
  • Insights are worked out in your browser from the notes you saved. Nothing is sent anywhere to make them.
  • Google Calendar, only if you connect it: Vaani reads your next 36 hours of events straight from Google into this browser. Nothing goes to any Vaani server. Disconnect any time. Access is read-only and the token is kept in memory only. Titles, times and call links are kept here for 36 hours, and removed the next time you open Vaani after that. Vaani reads attendee names, never their email addresses. The names stay on this device unless you turn on “Use attendee first names to write better notes” in Settings, Calendar. It is off until you turn it on. With it on, when your own AI key writes the notes, what Vaani reads as the first name of each person invited goes to that provider with the transcript (never an email address; a name Vaani is unsure of is skipped), so each task gets the right owner. A note you take for an event keeps its title, which goes to your AI provider like any note title if you use your own key. That note also keeps the event’s attendee names and the event id; backups leave them out unless you turn on “Include names and email addresses in backups” in Settings. If you use “Join and take notes” with the Chrome extension, the event’s title and attendee names are kept in this browser for up to 15 minutes so the Vaani window beside the call can use them. Disconnecting deletes the saved events and asks Google to remove Vaani’s access. If the sign-in has expired, Vaani asks Google for a short-lived token only to revoke it. If Google can’t do that without your sign-in, Vaani tells you, and you can remove access yourself in your Google Account under Security, Third-party connections (myaccount.google.com/connections).
  • No accounts, cookies for tracking, analytics or ads.

Your choices

  • Turn off the call prompt in the extension’s menu, or remove the extension at any time.
  • Delete any note from inside Vaani.
  • Disconnect Google Calendar in Settings at any time. You can also remove Vaani in your Google Account under Security, Third-party connections (myaccount.google.com/connections).
