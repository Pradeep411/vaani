    Use Sarvam AI for speech to text?

    Sarvam AI can turn speech into text with your key. Your meeting audio and your Names and words list then go from this browser to Sarvam AI. When Vaani hears a call through its Chrome extension, other people’s voices go too. Nothing goes to Vaani.

    Where your words go

    Your notes stay on this device. Here is every stop, plainly.

    Speech to text. Without a Sarvam AI key, your browser does it: Chrome and Edge send the audio to Google or Microsoft; Safari uses Apple’s engine. With a Sarvam AI key, the meeting audio goes from this browser straight to Sarvam AI, in pieces of about 25 seconds, to be turned into text, together with your Names and words list so names come out right. A piece with no sound in it is not sent, and the note says how much that was. When Vaani hears a call through its Chrome extension, the other people’s voices go there too. Your browser’s own speech recognition can only hear your mic, so without a Sarvam AI key Vaani keeps that recording’s audio in the tab (in memory, never saved or sent) until you close it, so you can turn the whole recording into notes later or save it as a file. Uploading a recording works the same way: with a key, its sound goes to Sarvam AI in pieces of under 25 seconds. Nothing goes to Vaani: the audio is held only in memory and dropped once it is transcribed. An uploaded file is never saved.
    Notes stay on this device. Everything you save lives in this browser’s storage (local storage, or IndexedDB when that is full). Clearing browser data deletes it.
    The built-in notes engine runs on your device. If you add your own AI key in Settings, text goes to that provider whenever Vaani writes notes, answers a question, recaps your week, translates or drafts a follow-up. With the transcript go the meeting’s date and length, the notes you typed while recording, the times you marked and your first name from Settings, so the notes get dates and owners right. If you picked an earlier meeting under “Follow-up to”, that meeting’s title and its open tasks go along too. Ask sends your question with the few passages of your notes and transcripts that match it (at most 8); Recap sends that week’s notes. OpenAI, Claude and Gemini get text only; a Sarvam AI key also turns on Sarvam AI’s speech to text, so Sarvam AI gets the audio too.
    Google Calendar, only if you connect it. Vaani reads your next 36 hours of events straight from Google into this browser. None of it goes to a Vaani account or database. Disconnect any time. Vaani reads attendee names, never their email addresses. The names stay on this device unless you turn on “Use attendee first names to write better notes” in Settings. It is off until you do. With it on, when your own AI key writes the notes, what Vaani reads as the first name of each person invited goes to that provider with the transcript (a name it is unsure of is skipped), so each task gets the right owner. You type the people a follow-up email goes to. An event’s title becomes the note’s title, which goes to your AI provider like any note title if you use your own key. A note you take for an event keeps the attendee names; backups leave them out unless you turn them on in Settings. Disconnect deletes the saved events and asks Google to remove Vaani’s access. If Google can’t, Vaani tells you, and you can remove it in your Google Account under Security, Third-party connections.
    After a meeting. Vaani drafts the follow-up email, the tasks and the follow-up meeting in this browser. Gmail, your mail app, WhatsApp and Google Calendar open only when you tap, filled in for you to check and send. Slack gets the notes only if you add a Slack channel link in Settings: when you tap, or on its own after each meeting if you choose “Do it automatically”.
    The Chrome extension. On Google Meet, Teams and Zoom pages it checks only whether a call is live (the hang-up button, whether the page plays a live call stream and, on Google Meet, whether a participant tile is on the page: a yes or no). It reads no names, chat or captions, and sends nothing anywhere. When you start notes from its icon, Chrome hands the call tab’s sound to the Vaani window beside the call, only until you stop.
